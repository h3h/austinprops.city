Proposition A asks voters to let the city borrow up to **$260 million** in
[general obligation bonds][LB] for park, recreation, aquatic and open space
improvements, including buying new parkland. The ballot labels it a property
tax increase, because the city repays bonds with property taxes.

The city published a list of the kinds of projects it has in mind, but the list
is not binding. Per reporting from [Austin Current][AC] and the
[Austin Parks Foundation][APF], it includes:

* Recreation buildings like Gus Garcia Recreation Center, Doris Miller
  Auditorium and Mayfield Park
* Improvements at Walter E. Long and Onion Creek metro parks, plus Jamestown and
  Bolm parks
* Infrastructure at Zilker, Mount Bonnell, Circle C and Evergreen Cemetery
* Upgrades at four pools
* New parks and trail connections in parts of town that don’t have many parks

## What it costs

City staff estimate that Props A and B together would add roughly **$40 a year**
to the city tax bill on a median-valued home (about $485,000), once all of the
debt is issued ([Austin Current][AC], [Community Impact][CI]). The money would
be borrowed over several years, so the increase phases in.

## The case against

Council put this on the ballot 8–3. Mayor Kirk Watson and Council Members José
Velásquez and Marc Duchen voted no, citing affordability and the amount of
money from earlier bonds that is still unspent ([KUT][KUT]). City financial
staff had recommended waiting until 2028. And [KXAN reported][KX] that the city
has cancelled more than 60 projects that voters already approved in past bonds.

That’s a fair criticism of how the city manages its bond programs. It isn’t a
reason to let pools and recreation centers keep falling apart. Bonds pay for
buildings and land that last decades, which is what borrowing is for, and the
cost per household is small.

**Recommendation:** Yes
