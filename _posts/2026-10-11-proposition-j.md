Proposition J fixes typos, punctuation and sentence structure in the City
Charter. It also changes or removes language that no longer has any effect
because state law or a final court order has replaced it. This is a
[charter amendment][LC] that the City Council put on the ballot.

One vote covers all of the edits, and it’s up to the council’s lawyers to say
which ones are truly “non-substantive.” That’s worth some skepticism. The city
has not published a short, readable list of every change, and we couldn’t find
one.

Still, language that state law or a court has already overridden can’t be
enforced, and keeping it in the charter only misleads people who read it.
Austin voters passed a similar cleanup in 2018 ([Prop I](/2018/proposition-i)).

**Recommendation:** Yes
