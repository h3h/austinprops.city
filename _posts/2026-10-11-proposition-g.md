Proposition G changes when the city holds elections on measures that residents
put on the ballot by petition, including petitioned
[charter amendments][LC]. Today they go on the next available election date.
Under Prop G they would go on the next November ballot in an even-numbered
year, when there is also a presidential or governor’s race. The City Council
put this on the ballot.

## The case for

Even-year November elections draw far more voters than odd-year or May
elections. A measure that changes how the city works should be decided by as
many Austinites as possible, not by whoever turns out for a low-turnout
election. It also means fewer stand-alone city elections to pay for.

## The case against

It can delay a petition measure by up to two years. Groups that use petitions,
including [Save Austin Now][SAN], the group behind
[Proposition I](/2026/proposition-i), argue that this weakens the main tool
residents have to check the City Council ([Austin Free Press][AFP]). Council
measures don’t face the same wait, which feels lopsided.

The same idea was on the 2024 ballot before a court cancelled that charter
election over an Open Meetings Act problem ([Austin Monitor][AM]).

## Our take

This is the closest call on the ballot. A two-year delay is real, but most
petition measures aren’t urgent. They’re about the long-term rules of the city,
and those should be set by the biggest electorate we can get.

**Recommendation:** Yes
