Proposition B asks voters to let the city borrow up to **$35 million** in
[general obligation bonds][LB] for libraries. Like Prop A, the ballot labels it
a property tax increase.

Per [KUT][KUT] and the city’s [bond engagement site][SU], the money would go to
two projects:

* About **$20 million** to renovate and expand the Hampton Branch at Oak Hill
* About **$15 million** for a new library branch in Colony Park, paired with a
  public health center

Colony Park, in far northeast Austin, has been waiting a long time for city
investment. A library and health clinic are about as clear a public good as a
city can buy.

The cost is included in the roughly $40 a year estimated for Props A and B
together on a median-valued home. In tax rate terms, that’s a little under
**1 cent per $100** of property value for both bonds, and about **0.1 cents per
$100** for Prop B on its own. (That’s our math: the $40 estimate divided by the
median home value, with Prop B’s share based on its $35 million out of the
$295 million total.) See [Proposition A](/2026/proposition-a) for the sources
behind that estimate and for the arguments against this year’s bonds.

**Recommendation:** Yes
